# Baseline, 2026-10-18, past the ledger's bound (W24)

The fifth baseline. The [fourth](2026-10-18-w22.md) bounded `Mounted.dispatched` to the most recent 1,000 exits but could not show it: at 1,000 updates nothing has been dropped yet, and the 3,000-update run had not finished. This file measures 200, 1,000 and 3,000 updates and explains the run that did not finish. The earlier files are not edited.

## Why the 3,000-update run did not finish

It was the harness, not the product. For every update the harness clicked and then waited with `setTimeout(0)`; a browser clamps nested timers to 4 ms, and the run takes a median of seven fresh browsers for each of three builds (counter installed, counter deduplicated, `docs-app`), each measured unthrottled and under 4x CPU throttling. That is about 1 second of waiting per update summed over the run, so 1,000 updates took over an hour and 3,000 would take three. The cost per update the *page* pays is flat (the table below), so there is no superlinear cost in Valance, the runtime or the PORT, and no browser-free reproduction was needed (`evidence/language/replay.mjs` already replays 1,000 updates in constant time per update).

The fix is in `scripts/measure-footprint.mjs`: the update loop waits on a `MessageChannel` message (no clamp), and `--runs N` and `--only counter` bound a run. Not shown: I did not re-run the old loop at 3,000 to confirm its duration; the account above is from reading the loop and from the 1,000-update run of the previous session.

## How it was measured

As in the fourth baseline: the workspace's `cli`, `common`, `valance` and `router` packed and installed as tarballs beside the published `mesh-runtime` 0.12.0, `nexus` 0.12.3 and `port-web` 0.6.1; counter and `docs-app`, both `vlx build --web`; Chromium 141 through `playwright-core`; JavaScript heap after a forced collection; WebAssembly memory over the exported memories. **Medians of 3 fresh browsers, not 7** (`--runs 3`, to fit the time). The tree is the Valance commit that adds this file, on `claude/wp2b-bridge`. Three runs: `--updates 200`, `--updates 1000`, `--updates 3000`.

## JavaScript heap after a forced collection (unthrottled, median of 3)

| | At start | After 200 | After 1,000 | After 3,000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Counter, installed build | 5,346,752 | 4,454,196 | 5,054,824 | 5,104,484 |
| Counter, deduplicated build | 5,378,464 | 4,454,124 | 5,053,700 | 5,105,060 |
| `docs-app` | 4,757,540 | 4,687,084 | 5,209,476 | 5,128,016 |

Under 4x throttling after 3,000 updates: counter 5,157,520 (installed) and 5,163,204 (deduplicated), `docs-app` 5,040,644.

**Is the heap flat after the bound? Yes.** Between 200 and 1,000 updates the heap rises about 600,000 bytes (the ledger filling to its bound: about 750 bytes an update on the counter, 650 on `docs-app`). Between 1,000 and 3,000 updates it moves by +49,660 bytes on the counter (+1.0 percent, 25 bytes an update, inside the 1 percent spread between the runs' own ranges, 5,096,384 to 5,113,328) and by −81,460 on `docs-app` (−1.6 percent; its range at 3,000 is 5,062,364 to 5,136,472). Before the bound, 2,000 more updates would have added about 300,000 bytes (the third baseline's 150 bytes an update). WebAssembly memory is exactly flat at every count: counter 1,310,720 and `docs-app` 2,228,224 bytes.

## Limits

One machine, one browser, medians of 3. The 200-update counter figure is 0.7 percent higher than the fourth baseline's (4,454,196 against 4,423,536), from the median of 3 against 7 or from the code since; it is under the fourth baseline's ceiling of 4,644,800.
