# Baseline, 2026-10-24, after WP8

The seventh baseline: the same harness, machine and settings as [the one before WP8](2026-10-24-wp8-before.md) (`--tree --runs 3 --updates 200`, medians of 3 fresh browsers), on the tree at the tip of `claude/wp8-budgets`. The raw results of both runs are beside the files (`2026-10-24-wp8-before.json`, `2026-10-24-wp8-after.json`). `scripts/check-budgets.mjs --results after.json --against before.json` passes: every ceiling holds, the ratio gates hold, and the two known reds are reported.

What the numbers do and do not say. The cost of WP8 in bytes is about 0.6 percent of the JavaScript (the client half of the early-input replay, `early-replay`, and the late admission's per-view bookkeeping); the page's HTML gains the shell (1,133 bytes, 655 gzipped, and a `<link>` or two), which is not counted above. The interactive-at rows fell for the counter (the module compiles while the script downloads, W11) and rose for `docs-app` at 4x CPU; with medians of 3 on a shared four-core container, 10 percent either way is within what the machine does to itself (the first baseline measured up to 16 percent), so **no start-up claim is made beyond "no worse"**, and the gate that holds it is the 2x ratio. First paint is noise at this resolution. Not measured: a throttled network (the prototype of Bet 3 measured 6 to 22 percent faster across six profiles; the harness has no network throttle), and `docs-app`'s first paint.

## A finding the heap gate made

The first run after the late admission was built was over the heap ceiling (4,666,024 against 4,630,800, 2 percent). The cause was not the code size but a stream: `SubscriptionRef.changes` merged into the render stream held about 85 kB of Effect machinery, once per mount. Late admission now wakes the stream with the state's own commit and a counter, and the heap is 4,584,224 (core only: 5,029,608 against 5,023,756 on the old core in the same page and script). This is the gate doing its job.
